# Science sources

The single register of every science claim the first-person 3D client shows the player (`crates/bevy-client`), and of every number in its rules engine that drives a shown outcome. `npm test` (`test/science-sources.test.ts`) fails if a client string that looks like a science claim has no row here, if a row is malformed or cites an undefined source, if a `_C` temperature constant in `chem.rs` has no row or a different value, or if a `C-` row's text is no longer in a file it names. The fresh-model science review that scores bar B6 follows `docs/loop/science-review.md`.

**What the lint sees.** The lint flags a client string as a claim when it has a temperature, a chemical formula, a science keyword, or the shape of a sentence (three or more words ending in `.`, `!`, `?` or `…`); everything else, including strings built at runtime and the skipped machine-output lines, is covered by the review protocol (see [Lint limits](../docs/loop/science-review.md#lint-limits)).

**Conditions.** Phase-change temperatures are at 101.325 kPa (1 atm). Standard enthalpies (ΔH°) are at 298.15 K and 1 bar, per mole of reaction as written. Temperatures shown to the player are rounded to the nearest whole degree (or kelvin).

**Verdicts.**

- `correct`: the statement shown to the player is true as stated, at the conditions in the register header, within the tolerance rules.
- `simplified`: the statement is true but idealized or incomplete (it omits a mechanism, rounds, or describes a limiting case), and no reasonable reading of it is false. The note says what is simplified and why it is not false.
- `error`: a statement shown to the player that is false, or a parameter that drives a shown outcome and lies outside every cited sourced range. The note gives the correct statement or value with its source.

**Adding a row.** Copy the exact in-game text into the `claim` cell inside single backticks (keep placeholders such as `{who}` and `${...}` literally; write a literal `|` as `\|`). Give it the next unused `C-NNN` id (ids are never reused), the repo-relative file(s) that show it, a verdict derived from the cited source, a note unless the verdict is `correct`, and one or more `[S<n>]` sources defined below. A new `_C` constant in `chem.rs` needs a `## Parameters` row with the same value. `[S0]` may be cited only in `## Parameters`, with a note that starts `gameplay tuning` or `scenario assumption`. Any item that adds or changes a player-facing science string or a `_C` constant updates this file in the same PR.

## Block world

Material rows for the I-052 block world (`content/materials.json`). Each row's `source` field names one heading below (`realgame-voxel` test V10 checks that every row has one). Values are SI at 20 °C and 1 atm unless the row says otherwise; `null` in the JSON means the field does not apply in play. `hardness_s` (dig time) and `burn_s` (how long a block burns) are gameplay tuning [S0], not physical claims; 9999 s marks a fixture that cannot be dug. Specific heats, conductivities and densities are taken from the cited tables, picking one value inside each cited range.

### mat-air
Air: density 1.204 kg/m³ (ideal gas, M = 28.97 g/mol [S12] [S8]); c_p 1.007 kJ/(kg·K) [S11]; k 0.0262 W/(m·K) [S33]. Air cells hold no heat in the sim; they are the room every block cools toward.

### mat-dirt
Dirt and grass: density 1200 kg/m³ (earth, loose [S34]); c_p 920 J/(kg·K) (clay [S32]); k 0.5 W/(m·K) (dry soil [S33]). Simplified: soil varies widely with water content.

### mat-sand
Sand: density 1500 kg/m³ (dry sand 1400-1600 [S34]); c_p 800 J/(kg·K) [S32]; k 0.2 W/(m·K) (0.15-0.25 [S33]); melts at 1713 °C, the melting point of silica [S35], into glass (P-026).

### mat-stone
Stone, cliffstone, runestone and the gas vent's casing: granite, density 2700 kg/m³ (2600-2800 [S34]); c_p 790 J/(kg·K) [S32]; k 2.8 W/(m·K) (1.7-4.0 [S33]). Runestone and vents are fixtures (cannot be dug).

### mat-wood
Wood, planks, leaves, torches and both benches: oak, density 720 kg/m³ [S34]; c_p 2000 J/(kg·K) [S32]; k 0.16 W/(m·K) (hardwoods [S33]); ignites at 300 °C [S37] [S6] (as P-004). Simplified: leaves are given wood's values (real leaves are lighter and ignite between 227 and 453 °C [S16]).

### mat-snow
Snow: density 100 kg/m³ [S34]; c_p 2090 J/(kg·K) (ice [S32]); k 0.15 W/(m·K) (0.05-0.25 [S33]); melts at 0 °C [S10].

### mat-ice
Ice: density 917 kg/m³ [S34]; c_p 2090 J/(kg·K) [S32] [S11]; k 2.18 W/(m·K) [S33]; melts at 0 °C [S10], taking 334 kJ/kg (P-031).

### mat-water
Liquid water: density 998 kg/m³ at 20 °C [S39]; c_p 4184 J/(kg·K) [S11]; k 0.606 W/(m·K) [S33]; freezes at 0 °C and boils at 100 °C [S2] [S10].

### mat-steam
Steam: density 0.59 kg/m³ at 100 °C (ideal gas, ρ = pM/RT with M = 18.02 g/mol [S12]); c_p 1864 J/(kg·K) (water vapour [S11]); k 0.0267 W/(m·K) [S33].

### mat-mud
Mud: wet clay, density 1800 kg/m³ (clay 1800-2600 [S34]); c_p 920 J/(kg·K) (clay [S32]); k 1.0 W/(m·K) (clay 0.15-1.8 [S33]). Simplified: one value for a mix that varies with its water.

### mat-coal
Coal ore: anthracite, density 1400 kg/m³ (1400-1800 [S34]); c_p 1260 J/(kg·K) [S32]; k 0.2 W/(m·K) [S33]. Simplified: coal seams do not catch fire from a short flame in play (flammability 0; bituminous coal glows from 454 °C and anthracite from 600 °C [S37]). Coal is the carbon that reduces iron ore (P-027) and the fuel the Invention Bench burns.

### mat-iron-ore
