# I-052 critique (self-critique of spec.md, first draft)

Three readers: a 6-year-old (Sprout), a 13-year-old (Apprentice), and an assignment judge. Each finding has a verdict: **fix** (changed in the revised spec), **keep** (with the reason), or **note** (for later).

## 6-year-old (Sprout, cannot read well, plays with a mouse)

1. **"Hold the Pick, key 5" and keys 6-9 are too many numbers.** A 6-year-old already struggles with 1-4. Digging needs its own key, and so does choosing a block. A "hold to dig with any spell" shortcut was considered and rejected, because it mixes casting and digging under one button. **Fix:** the Pick gets a big hotbar picture and the objective at step 4 shows the Pick picture. Placing blocks is not needed on the Must path at all, so key 6-9 placing is optional free play. The revised spec says so, and the step-4 objective for Sprout is a picture of the Pick and the coal.
2. **The intro is three cards of text.** **Fix:** Sprout hears every card read aloud (already in the spec) and each card has a big picture (the cold Kiln, a snowflake on the Kiln, the apprentice). Any click skips. The skip appears after 1 s, so a child who clicks at once does not miss everything; the first card holds 3 s at Sprout.
3. **The Invention Bench asks a 6-year-old to "invent".** Choosing 1-3 items from a pack is fine with pictures, but a fizzle reason in words is lost on her. **Fix:** at Sprout, the bench shows two big glowing cards it suggests (Coal + O2, or H2 + O2) once she has them, and a picture of a sad, cold Kiln for a wrong mix. She still picks them herself. The reaction text is spoken.
4. **The deep-water push-back could feel like a bug.** **Fix:** the push-back plays a splash and a shiver sound, the screen edges frost for 1 s, and the Sprout caption picture is the snowflake icon (the existing "freeze" Sprout picture, I-035).
5. **She will get lost.** The arrow helps. **Keep**, and add: the objective arrow at Sprout is always on (not only when the target is off screen), as a small chevron at the screen edge or a bobbing marker over the target when it is in view.
6. **Dark cave can scare a 6-year-old.** **Fix:** the cave is dim, never black (ambient floor 120 rather than 40), crystals glow brighter at Sprout, and the music there is calm, not ominous.

## 13-year-old (Apprentice, has played Minecraft)

1. **"It's Minecraft without the fun parts."** No crafting tree, one recipe (Torch). A 13-year-old will open the pack and want to make things. **Fix:** move the Furnace from Should to Must-light: a Furnace block you craft from 8 stone, which turns sand into glass and iron ore plus coal into iron when you light it with Fire and feed it O2 or coal. It reuses matter's glass and iron rules (M8), so it costs little beyond a recipe and a block. Glass and iron blocks are then placeable. That also makes the "sand + heat → glass" and "ore + charcoal → iron" rules visible in play instead of only in tests.
2. **The puzzles are "cast spell X at Y".** A Minecraft player will try to dig around the ice gate and bridge the river with dirt. The protected boxes stop that, but a wall that "won't budge" is a cheap answer. **Keep** the protected boxes (without them the puzzles are not puzzles), but **fix** the feel: protected blocks look different (carved runestone tiles with a faint glow in the atlas), so the rule is learnt by sight, not by failing.
3. **The river freezes from a set number of casts.** That is a counting puzzle. **Keep**, because it is real (heat removed per cast), but the spec's matter rules make it richer: water flowing in from upstream re-warms thin ice, so freezing at the narrow ford is the smart move. **Note:** ensure the ford's water is still enough that the bridge holds (the spec already keeps the ford below 0 °C); state it as a stamp rule.
4. **Free play after the ending is empty.** **Note:** "Keep exploring" with no goal is fine for the assignment. Later items can add creatures and more biomes.
5. **CO2 being invisible.** Pooling CO2 is the coolest science here, and a 13-year-old wants to see it. The spec has translucent puffs above 0.3 fraction. **Fix:** make the pooled CO2 a low white mist layer drawn as a flat translucent sheet at the top of the pooled gas cells in the hollow (one quad per gas column, at most 64), so it looks like dry-ice fog and you can see it fill and spill.
6. **Typing `C + O2 -> CO2` at the Invention Bench is good.** **Keep.** A wrong product like `CO` should get the same kind reasons as the Formula Bench (it is a real reaction with less oxygen, so the reason must not call it "impossible"). **Fix:** C4 is reworded: `C + O2 -> CO` is refused as unbalanced (the bench's existing `atoms_off` code), with the reason the parser already gives.

## Assignment judge (Game Week, Option 1)

1. **Beginning, middle, end: met.** Intro, 7 steps, an ending card. **Keep.**
2. **3+ connected, revisitable areas: met, but revisiting has no reason.** The judge may ask why you would go back. **Fix:** the route goes back through the Village on purpose. The cave's ladder exit and the shortcut door return you home, and the Kiln is in the Village. Also, the coal seam is behind the ice gate, and the Furnace (now Must-light) is in the Village, so a player who wants glass walks back with sand from the Marsh. The revised section 7 states the revisit reasons instead of the confused paragraph about vents.
3. **3+ real puzzles: met (5).** But P2 (ford + gate) and P3 (hollow) are both "cast the right spell at the thing". The judge's word is "real puzzle". **Fix:** P4 is a true puzzle (choose the gas: O2 sinks, H2 lifts) and P5 asks you to combine. For P3, add a small twist that the matter sim gives for free: the hollow has a gap in its rim on one side, so CO2 spills out unless you first **place one block** (or Frost the shallow water there into an ice dam) to close the gap. It uses the block world and the chemistry together, and the wrong order (CO2 first) visibly spills. Protected boxes leave exactly that one gap cell placeable. This replaces a pure "3 casts" puzzle with a two-step one. Unit test M4 is extended (M4b: with the gap open, the vents stay lit after 3 casts; with it closed, they go out).
4. **Progression where an item opens new paths: met** (coal → torch → cave; Spark → Kiln door). **Keep.**
5. **Polished, cohesive generated visuals.** A generated 16 px atlas can look like a cheap Minecraft copy. The toon look the target asks for (low-poly toon) is a separate style. **Fix:** the atlas is generated in the game's existing palette (the colours of `StateMats` and the sky `ClearColor`), with flat colour tiles plus a thin darker border per block rather than noisy textures, so it reads as a "toon block" style, consistent with the old courtyard. Vertex AO gives the depth.
6. **Generated SFX and music: met** (WebAudio synthesis). **Note:** the judge may expect "generated" to mean made with a generative tool. Procedural synthesis is generated audio, and the ElevenLabs connector this project has cannot make music. The spec says so in one line so the submission text can say "procedurally generated".
7. **Playable by a stranger: the age chooser before the intro is a hurdle.** **Keep** the chooser (bands are how the game teaches), but **fix:** the chooser has a "Just play" default (Seedling) that is focused, so Enter or a click starts.
8. **10-15 minutes: plausible.** The run has 4 bench builds. The panel timing on Wednesday is the check. **Fix:** add a measured rule to the Wednesday panel: median first run between 10 and 20 min for the 9- and 13-year-old personas; if it is over 20, shorten the walking route, not the puzzles.
9. **Risk the judge sees half a game.** If the engine slips, the whole thing fails. **Fix:** add an explicit fallback cut list with dates (Monday night checkpoint already exists): Tuesday noon, if E9-E12 are not green, the gas field is replaced by the per-hollow counter; Wednesday noon, if the run is not playable end to end, the cave becomes one room with the rig and the ledge, and the Furnace is cut again.

4. The mastery change belongs where builders and Paawan will see it. **Keep** in section 2 and 7; also add it to the summary for the coordinator.
